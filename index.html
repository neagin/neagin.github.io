About

I'm a PhD candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University, where I work with Prof. Tommaso Melodia at the Institute for Intelligent Networked Systems.

My research brings machine learning into the lowest layers of the wireless stack, where decisions have to land within microseconds. I like building on real radios: GPU-accelerated 5G pipelines on NVIDIA Aerial, mmWave testbeds, and O-RAN dApps that sit right next to the PHY.

This past summer I was at Nokia Bell Labs, working on RAN pipelines that adapt to the applications they serve. Back at Northeastern, I'm taking my interference work from detection to mitigation, and training channel estimators on over-the-air data.

Research

Most of my work asks one question: how much intelligence can you fit into a radio's real-time loop before you miss the deadline?

InterfO-RAN

ACM MobiHoc 2025 · U.S. patent pending. Read the paper

Uplink interference is easy to notice and hard to catch in time. InterfO-RAN is the first GPU-accelerated O-RAN dApp: it runs alongside the NVIDIA Aerial GPU-accelerated PHY inside an O-RAN-compliant gNB, and uses a convolutional neural network to detect in-band uplink interference as it happens.

I collected the interference and interference-free datasets, trained the model, brought it into Aerial through ONNX Runtime, and optimized it with TensorRT. Trained and tested on more than 7 million 5G NR uplink slots from real deployments, it detects interference with over 91% accuracy in about 650 µs on a commercial-grade private 5G network.

ARCHES

arXiv preprint, 2026. Read the paper

AI models in the PHY aren't free: they cost GPU time and power on every slot. ARCHES switches between AI and conventional channel estimation in a GPU-accelerated 5G pipeline in real time, using cross-layer telemetry to decide when inference is worth running.

A CUDA switch kernel and a dApp control path close the loop in 140 µs end to end. The result is 5–7% higher uplink throughput and 9.6% lower GPU power, at the same time.

We showed ARCHES running live at MWC 2026, as a Northeastern and SoftBank demo at the AI-RAN Alliance booth. Watch the demo

From detection to mitigation

In progress

Detecting interference is only half the job. I'm now extending InterfO-RAN into a mitigation framework: an xApp groups UEs by the interference conditions they experience, and a dApp adapts the gNB's PRB allocations in real time to steer them away from interfered resources.

AI channel estimation from over-the-air data

In progress

I'm training a deep-learning channel estimator for the 5G NR uplink (PUSCH) on over-the-air data from an NVIDIA Aerial testbed, and judging it the way a receiver would: end to end through equalization, LDPC decoding, and code-block CRC checks.

The NI mmWave Transceiver System came as a PHY-only platform. I built a retransmission-based MAC layer on top of it, turning it into an end-to-end programmable testbed that streams video reliably in real time.

5G NR initial access

Texas A&M University, with Prof. P. R. Kumar

Interference-free cloud RAN

IIT Hyderabad and Tejas Networks, with Prof. Kiran Kuchi

A 4G cloud RAN prototype that cancels interference using downlink coordinated multipoint transmission, TDD channel calibration, and Tomlinson–Harashima precoding at both transmitter and receiver, running on a full 6×6 base station and UE setup. I designed the pilot and data transmission over LTE OFDM frames. The project won three gold medals at the India International Innovation Fair in 2016 and was exhibited at Rashtrapati Bhavan in 2017.
